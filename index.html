Map colors and pressure interpretation

Green: more aerated. Color intensity follows the unit’s open fraction. Green describes aeration; it does not certify healthy or safely ventilated tissue.

Grey: less aerated. These units have a low modeled open fraction. The model does not show actual CT density or predict oxygenation.

Orange: intratidal aeration change. The unit’s open fraction changes between expiration and inspiration. This is a recruitment-change marker, not a direct measure of injury.

Red: high volume ratio. Shown at inspiration when the unit exceeds the assumed distension ratio. Select end inspiration to see it. The threshold is illustrative.

Airway pressure includes the elastic loads of the lung and chest wall. Pleural pressure represents the modeled surrounding thoracic pressure. Transpulmonary pressure = airway − pleural pressure; it describes the pressure across lung tissue. Higher transpulmonary pressure can recruit closed tissue and can also increase distension of open tissue.

Interpreting an adjustment: more aeration may distribute tidal volume across more tissue, while higher PEEP may also distend tissue that is already open. Compliance and driving pressure describe global mechanics; the regional map helps expose that tradeoff.

The curve shows the airway pressure required for a given total gas volume. A steeper curve means more pressure per added volume. The sweep compares aeration and the distension proxy across PEEP. Upward and downward paths can differ because recruitment retains pressure history. Active patients are preserved. Outlined markers indicate inspiratory pressure-ceiling exposure; check delivery before comparing tissue fractions.

Teaching questions

At a fixed tidal volume, predict what will happen to driving pressure when PEEP rises. Then compare the two patients.

Uncheck Fresh patients, raise PEEP, then return it to the starting value. Does the retained recruitment state return to its previous value? Compare this with resetting the patients.

Compare the higher chest-wall load phenotype with the higher recruitability phenotype using the same seed. Which pressure describes the load on lung tissue?

Increase tidal volume. Compare the delivered volume, driving pressure and distension proxy. Can global compliance alone exclude increased regional distension?

What the checks establish

Numerical verification

Volume residuals, pressure-limit handling, monotonic pressure-volume behavior, reproducible seeds, and timestep refinement.

Physiological plausibility

Recruitment history, different PEEP responses, chest-wall effects, and regional distension. These checks support the teaching model's internal behavior.

External validation remains open

No CT, EIT, patient waveform, or experimental lung dataset has calibrated this release. Simulated outputs do not identify a patient's best PEEP.

Performance has a scope

Measured engine latency depends on hardware, browser, unit count, and timestep. Mobile layout tests do not measure an actual phone's CPU.

MODEL DOCUMENTATION

Model equations and assumptions

The lung is a weighted set of heterogeneous regional units. Each unit retains an open fraction across breaths. Its nonlinear pressure-volume curve stiffens at higher distension. A shared chest wall couples the units.
