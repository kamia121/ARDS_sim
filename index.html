What am I looking at?

Green — ventilated areas. Open, air-filled lung regions, shown as ventilated here. Ventilated does not mean healthy; an open region can still overstretch.

Grey — poorly aerated areas. Less lung is available to share the breath. Watch whether these areas open as pressure rises.

Orange — recruitment during the breath. An orange ring appears as a region gains aeration during inspiration. Watch whether more PEEP keeps them open between breaths.

Red — possible overstretch. At end inspiration, these open regions exceed the model’s distension threshold. More lung can open while other regions stretch more.

Pressure across the lung = airway − pleural pressure. The chest wall can raise airway pressure without an equal rise in lung-distending pressure.

Teaching questions

At a fixed tidal volume, predict what will happen to driving pressure when PEEP rises. Then compare the two patients.

Uncheck Fresh patients, raise PEEP, then return it to the starting value. Does the retained recruitment state return to its previous value? Compare this with resetting the patients.

Compare the higher chest-wall load phenotype with the higher recruitability phenotype using the same seed. Which pressure describes the load on lung tissue?

Increase tidal volume. Compare the delivered volume, driving pressure and distension proxy. Can global compliance alone exclude increased regional distension?

What the checks establish

Numerical verification

Volume residuals, pressure-limit handling, monotonic pressure-volume behavior, reproducible seeds, and timestep refinement.

Physiological plausibility

Recruitment history, different PEEP responses, chest-wall effects, and regional distension. These checks support the teaching model's internal behavior.

External validation remains open

No CT, EIT, patient waveform, or experimental lung dataset has calibrated this release. Simulated outputs do not identify a patient's best PEEP.

Performance has a scope

Measured engine latency depends on hardware, browser, unit count, and timestep. Mobile layout tests do not measure an actual phone's CPU.

MODEL DOCUMENTATION

Model equations and assumptions

The lung is a weighted set of heterogeneous regional units. Each unit retains an open fraction across breaths. Its nonlinear pressure-volume curve stiffens at higher distension. A shared chest wall couples the units.

Equations and output definitions

For each region, open gas volume is its tissue weight times [rest volume + capacity × max(transpulmonary pressure, 0) / (stiffness + max(transpulmonary pressure, 0))]. Total gas volume sums each region’s open fraction times its open gas volume.

Regional pleural pressure = baseline pleural pressure + dependent gradient × regional position + chest-wall elastance × (total gas volume − chest-wall reference volume). Transpulmonary pressure = airway − regional pleural pressure. The displayed mean pleural pressure is a tissue-weighted spatial mean at the indicated phase.

Opening occurs above an assumed opening threshold; closure occurs below a separate closing threshold. For threshold excess x, the rate is x / (3 + x) / the opening or closing time constant. These recruitment time constants are not respiratory resistance × compliance time constants.

Delivered tidal volume is end-inspiratory volume minus volume before the final inspiration. Driving pressure is end-inspiratory airway pressure minus PEEP; respiratory compliance is delivered tidal volume divided by driving pressure. The distension proxy weights aerated tissue whose fully open inspiratory volume ratio exceeds 1.65 relative to that region at transpulmonary pressure 5 cmH2O. These parameters and thresholds are illustrative and uncalibrated.
