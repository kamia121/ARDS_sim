MODEL DOCUMENTATION

Model equations and assumptions

The recruitment reference uses weighted regions, pressure/time-dependent opening and a nonlinear pressure-volume curve. The airflow experiment freezes aeration at that reference and uses linear chord compliance, airway resistances and a shared chest wall.

Recruitment equations and output definitions

For each region, open gas volume is its tissue weight times [rest volume + capacity × max(transpulmonary pressure, 0) / (stiffness + max(transpulmonary pressure, 0))]. Total gas volume sums each region’s open fraction times its open gas volume.

Regional pleural pressure = baseline pleural pressure + dependent gradient × regional position + chest-wall elastance × (total gas volume − chest-wall reference volume). Transpulmonary pressure = airway − regional pleural pressure. The displayed mean pleural pressure is a tissue-weighted spatial mean at the indicated phase.

Opening occurs above an assumed opening threshold; closure occurs below a separate closing threshold. For threshold excess x, the rate is x / (3 + x) / the opening or closing time constant. These recruitment time constants are not respiratory resistance × compliance time constants.

Delivered tidal volume is end-inspiratory volume minus volume before the final inspiration. Driving pressure is end-inspiratory airway pressure minus PEEP; respiratory compliance is delivered tidal volume divided by driving pressure. The distension proxy weights aerated tissue whose fully open inspiratory volume ratio exceeds 1.65 relative to that region at transpulmonary pressure 5 cmH2O. These parameters and thresholds are illustrative and uncalibrated.

Unified experiment: flow chart and scope
  1. Ventilator request. Inspiration asks for a constant flow of tidal volume ÷ inspiratory time. If the effective pressure would pass the limit, the model holds the limit for the rest of inspiration. Expiration holds PEEP. No pause is simulated.
  2. Airway. Flow passes a shared central resistance, then each region’s own resistance, which scales with tissue weight and, if chosen, with spread, dependency and how open the region is.
  3. Gas volume. A region’s gas changes only by the flow into or out of it. Opening or closing tissue changes how that gas is held, not how much there is.
  4. Local pressure. Pressure inside a region’s airspace follows from its gas volume, its open fraction, a nonlinear pressure–volume law, and a smooth stiffening below its resting size.
  5. Pleural pressure. Pressure around each region = baseline + front-to-back gradient + chest-wall recoil from total lung gas, plus any posture or chest-load setting.
  6. Pressure across the region. Local alveolar minus local pleural pressure is compared with that region’s opening and closing thresholds. It sets how fast the open fraction changes.
  7. Feedback. A new open fraction changes the local pressure at the same gas, which changes flow. The steps repeat through ten breaths, so the last breath may not yet repeat the one before.

What is reported. Gas volume at the end of the breath; the resting volume the lung would settle at for the set PEEP if the end-of-breath open fractions were frozen; their signed difference (unfinished emptying, relative to that reference); each region’s local pressure above PEEP; delivered volume; peak airway pressure; opened and high-stretch tissue shares; and flags for the pressure limit, non-repeating breaths and numerical cautions.

What is not. There is no end-inspiratory or end-expiratory hold, so no plateau pressure, no driving pressure from a hold and no compliance are shown. The resting PEEP volume is not expected to equal the end-expiratory volume. The reference does not predict what a long exhalation would do, because opening would keep changing. Local pressure above PEEP is a model value, not a measured auto-PEEP. Nothing here is calibrated or validated, and no result is a treatment recommendation or evidence of injury.

Airflow experiment: assumptions and measures

Regional aeration is frozen at a prepared recruitment reference. Linear regional compliance is the chord from the relaxed PEEP volume to the frozen inspiratory reference. This isolates airflow and emptying; it does not dynamically recruit tissue, calculate injury, oxygenation or cardiovascular responses.

Flows follow pressure gradients through regional resistance Rᵢ = Rp / tissue weight and a shared central resistance R0. Regional elastic pressure and a shared chest wall couple the paths. Intrinsic regional RᵢCᵢ excludes shared resistance and chest wall; it is not the region’s observed network emptying time.

Fraction of excess gas emptied = (end-inspiratory excess volume − end-expiratory excess volume) / end-inspiratory excess volume, above the relaxed frozen PEEP reference. Regional fractions can fall outside 0–100% through redistribution. Hypothetical equilibration pressures are estimates, not measured plateau or intrinsic PEEP.

The assumed pressure limit switches to pressure control at the ceiling for the remainder of inspiration, rather than terminating the breath. Trials start relaxed and settle toward a periodic cycle with an explicit residual and cap. Negative absolute volume fails the trial; chord-range excursions are reported. Parameters are uncalibrated and not treatment recommendations.

Physiology references & what this model assumes

These studies inform the teaching concepts. The simulator’s regions, thresholds and parameters are illustrative and have not been fitted to these data.

  • Healthy lungs and dependent atelectasis (CT study): dependent aeration changes with anesthesia and ventilation. A healthy reference is not a claim that every region must stay open in every setting.
  • Grasso et al., 2008: positive versus negative-pressure ventilation: an experimental animal comparison. The model explores pressure partitioning, and does not reproduce the study’s oxygenation or injury outcomes.
  • Prone versus supine regional mechanics in healthy subjects: CT-based regional function differs by posture. Our schematic pleural gradient is an assumption, not a CT prediction.
  • Chest-wall loading in supine and prone COVID-19 ARDS: responses vary. Loading is not equivalent to simply making the lung stiffer, and this model does not convert kilograms to pleural pressure.
  • Yoshida et al., 2018: esophageal and regional pleural pressure: animal and cadaver measurements show regional differences. Simulated Pes samples the model pleural field at an assumed mid-dorsal level. It is not a balloon measurement or the whole-lung mean.
  • Chiumello et al., 2008: lung stress and strain: clinical measurements of stress and strain do not calibrate this model’s expansion colors.
  • Protti et al., 2011: experimental strain and injury: healthy-pig experiment using VT/FRC. Its thresholds do not establish our assumed Vopen/TP5 cutoff as injurious.
